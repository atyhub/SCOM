Is the discount worth a bigger order?

The supplier offers a lower unit price P when you order more. Each price gives its own total cost curve TC = (D/Q)S + (Q/2)IP + PD, but a curve only counts inside its feasible range of order sizes. The solid parts of the curves are what you can actually pay. The best order is the lowest point on that solid, stepped line.

Change a break point or a price and watch the solid segments and the optimum move. Breaks are sorted automatically; tier 1 always starts at 0.

The four steps

1. For each price, compute Q* = √(2DS / IP).
2. If Q* is below the tier's range, raise it to the lowest quantity that earns the price. If it is above the range, drop that tier.
3. Compute total cost at each remaining quantity.
4. Pick the lowest total cost.
