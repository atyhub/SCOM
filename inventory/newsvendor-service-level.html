Single-period inventory model

Also called the newsvendor problem. You order once, before demand is known, for one selling period: newspapers, fresh bakery goods, seasonal fashion, event merchandise. Leftover units are sold off at a salvage value or thrown away, and unmet demand is lost. The only decision is Q, how many units to stock.

Service level

The probability that demand during the period does not exceed Q, so no customer is turned away: SL = P(D ≤ Q). Raising Q raises the service level but leaves more unsold stock on average. For normal demand, Q = μ + Z·σ, and Z·σ is the safety stock.

Choosing Q with costs

Each unit short costs Cs, the cost of shortage (lost margin plus any goodwill loss). Each unit left over costs Co, the cost of overage (purchase cost minus salvage). The best Q sets the service level equal to the critical ratio Cs / (Cs + Co), which balances the two risks.
