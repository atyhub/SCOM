What if the order arrives gradually?

In the EOQ model the whole order arrives at once. When we produce the item ourselves, units come in at the production rate p while customers keep using them at the demand rate d. Stock builds up at p − d during the run, so it never reaches Q. The peak is only Q(1 − d/p), which lowers holding cost and makes it worth running larger batches.

What to notice

Why the peak is lower. During a run lasting t = Q/p days, stock grows by p − d per day, so it peaks at (p − d)·Q/p = Q(1 − d/p). Average inventory is half of that peak.

Same balance rule. Q*p is where holding and setup cost are equal. Solving (Q/2)(1 − d/p)H = (D/Q)S gives Q*p = √(2DS / [H(1 − d/p)]).

Larger batches than EOQ. Because (1 − d/p) is below 1, each unit of Q costs less to hold, so Q*p is always larger than the EOQ and the total cost is lower. With the defaults, Q*p ≈ 283 versus EOQ = 200.

Play with p. Make p very large and production becomes almost instant: the profile turns into the EOQ sawtooth and Q*p falls back to the EOQ. Move p down towards d and the peak flattens, Q*p grows and production runs almost all the time.
