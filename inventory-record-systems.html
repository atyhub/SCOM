How do we know what's on the shelf?

Every reorder decision depends on knowing how much stock is really there. Record accuracy comes before inventory models, production schedules and, in the end, sales. There are three common ways to keep track. Pick one below, press play and watch what the manager knows compared with what is really on the shelf.

Count every P days, then order back up to a target level T

Someone walks the shelf at fixed intervals, counts what is there and orders enough to bring stock up to T. Small retailers use this, and so do vendor-managed arrangements, where the supplier's rep checks and restocks. The catch: between counts nobody is watching.

What to notice

  • The blue line is flat between counts. The real stock keeps falling, but the record doesn't change until the next count.
  • Make P longer and you need a bigger T (more stock) to avoid red stockout days. That extra stock is the price of less control.
  • Try P = 14 with T = 90 and watch the shelf run empty before anyone notices.

Two containers: when the first one is empty, order

Stock is split into two bins, and each bin holds enough to cover demand during the lead time. You use the first bin. When it is empty, that is your signal: send the order card and start on the second bin. The refill arrives before the second bin runs out. Nobody has to count anything.

What to notice

  • The empty bin is the reorder signal. It's a visual reorder point, with no counting and no computer.
  • Shrink the bins below d × L, or lengthen the lead time, and both bins empty before the delivery comes.
  • Common for cheap, fast-moving items: screws in a workshop, gloves in a clinic, Kanban bins on a line.

Every receipt and every sale updates the record, all the time

A bar code is scanned at receiving (+) and at the point of sale (−), so the record always shows current stock and the system can reorder by itself at the reorder point. But the record is only as good as its data. Theft, damage and mis-scans make it drift away from what is really on the shelf.

    What to notice

    • With losses switched off, the two lines are identical. That is the promise of a perpetual system: control at any moment.
    • With losses on, the record says stock is there when the shelf is empty (phantom stock). The system doesn't reorder in time and customers find nothing.
    • Press Cycle count now to correct the record. Accurate records need regular counting, even in a perpetual system.
