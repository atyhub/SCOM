From the Process to the Distribution

Each dot is one item leaving a process: a part cut to length, a pack filled to weight, any product with one measured characteristic. It is measured and lands in its place in the distribution. One item is unpredictable; thousands of items together form a bell curve with a mean μ and a standard deviation σ. Process and specification

Key ideas

One item is a surprise, many are a pattern

You cannot predict the next measurement, but you can predict the shape of the thousand items that follow: bell-shaped, centred on μ, with spread σ.

68 – 95 – 99.7

About 68 % of items fall within ±1σ, 95 % within ±2σ and 99.7 % within ±3σ. With few items the shares wobble; with thousands they settle on these values.

σ belongs to the process, the limits to the customer

The red zones come from the specification. To get fewer red dots you reduce σ, or move μ to the centre of the limits.

Common cause vs special cause

Normal variation is common-cause: it is always there. A shift in the mean (new tool, new supplier, a different operator) is a special cause: the run chart shows it, and the histogram no longer matches the curve.

Check your understanding

1. Add 20 items, then 2,000. Why does the small histogram look ragged and the large one match the curve?

A small sample is a lottery draw: chance moves the dots around. The curve describes the process, not a handful of items. As the number of items grows, the observed shares approach the theoretical ones (the cards for ±1σ, ±2σ, ±3σ move to 68, 95 and 99.7 %).

2. Load "Cp = 1.0", then "Cp = 1.33" and "Cp = 2.0". How many items per million are out of spec in theory?

For a centred process: about 2,700 (0.27 %) at Cp = 1.0 (3σ limits), 63 at Cp = 1.33 (4σ limits) and about 0.002 at Cp = 2.0 (6σ limits). Same specification, smaller σ.

3. Load "Cp = 1.0", run 1,000 items, then switch on the special cause and run 1,000 more. What changes in the run chart and in the histogram?

The points jump up by 1.5σ and many end up above the upper control line. The histogram gets wider and lopsided and no longer fits the curve. Out of spec rises to about 6.7 % (66,807 per million), the same value the Six Sigma convention gives for a 3σ process that drifts by 1.5σ.

4. Load "Off-centre". Cp looks excellent, so why are so many items red?

Cp only compares the width of the process with the width of the limits. The mean is close to the USL, so the nearest limit is only 2.25σ away: Cpk = 0.75 and about 1.2 % of items are out of spec. Re-centre the mean and the problem disappears without touching σ.
